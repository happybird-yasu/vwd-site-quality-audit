# Population Flow Map: Hokkaido (2019, 2021 and 2025)

Hokkaido is an island in the north of Japan, the largest island after Honshu. Its main city is Sapporo.

Snow-covered mountains and wide pastures; roads running straight through farmland, harbor towns by the sea, and the streets of Sapporo. Hokkaido has many different ways of life.

Some of you may have visited, or may have felt that you would like to live there someday.

The people who live in Hokkaido also move for reasons such as school, a new job, or family life.

In each of these towns and cities, where do people come from, and where do they go when they leave?

Why not trace the flows of people in each part of Hokkaido by following the map?

## Why We Made the Hokkaido Edition

“Are people gathering in Tokyo and Sapporo?” That question made us want to look more closely at the movement of people within Hokkaido.

Tokyo is the capital of Japan and Sapporo is the main city of Hokkaido. Besides the flow of people from Hokkaido toward these cities, what kinds of movement are there among the towns inside Hokkaido?

The Hokkaido edition looks at individual municipalities and lets you compare 2019, 2021 and 2025.

I also wanted to know what kinds of people are moving, so the map can also be switched by sex, nationality and age group.

## What I Noticed on the Map

What stood out to me on the map of Hokkaido was that there seemed to be more movement of people within Hokkaido than I had imagined.

I had the picture that people keep leaving Hokkaido for other areas. But on the map, I noticed not only people leaving from various places but also flows of people coming in.

Looking at the connections with places outside Hokkaido, and following the moves between towns inside Hokkaido, reveals movements of people that a one-way picture cannot show.

## Net Migration for the Whole of Hokkaido

How has the difference between people moving in and people moving out changed across the whole of Hokkaido?

The data card below compares net migration for 2019, 2021 and 2025.

【Net Migration Data Card: Hokkaido】

Net migration is the number of people who moved in minus the number who moved out. A positive figure means more people moved in than out (net inflow); a negative figure means more moved out than in (net outflow).

This card shows the difference in domestic moves between Hokkaido and places outside Hokkaido.

## About the Data

The totals and net migration figures shown here cover domestic moves by Japanese nationals and foreign nationals combined.

The map's age view uses four groups: 0–19, 20–39, 40–59 and 60 and over. People whose age is unknown are not included in these four groups.

This page is based on data published by the Statistics Bureau of Japan in its “Report on Internal Migration in Japan.” Visible World by Data compiled and tabulated the data and turned it into the map.

The years covered are 2019, 2021 and 2025. The Hokkaido edition of this site covers the municipalities within Hokkaido.

The migration shown here means people who changed their registered address across municipal or similar boundaries within Japan. It does not show people entering Japan from abroad or leaving Japan for other countries.

It also differs from commuting, travel or tourist crowds, and from population change that includes births and deaths. If the same person moved more than once during the period, each move is counted.

The municipality-level data on the map include moves within Hokkaido. In the city of Sapporo, moves to a different ward within the same city are also counted.

### Notes on Reading the Figures

- “What I Noticed on the Map” records impressions from looking at the map and the figures. It is not a statistically verified conclusion about the reasons for moving or about regional trends.
- Migration alone cannot tell you whether a region's total population went up or down.
- The rankings cover the municipalities and wards listed individually in the official table. Areas grouped under “others” and similar rows are not ranked, so this is not a complete ranking of all municipalities.
- The total shown is the official table's total, not the sum of the top-10 ranking.
- The nationality switch shows people who moved within Japan, by nationality. It does not show movement across national borders.
- Where municipal boundaries or classifications have changed, comparisons across years need care.
