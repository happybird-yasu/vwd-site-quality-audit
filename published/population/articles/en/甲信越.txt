# Population Flow Map: Koshinetsu (2019, 2021 and 2025)

The Koshinetsu region lies in the center of Honshu, Japan's largest island, and consists of three prefectures: Yamanashi, Nagano and Niigata. It stretches from the mountains west of Tokyo to Niigata on the Sea of Japan.

The high mountains known as the Japanese Alps, and towns in mountain valleys; orchards and farmland, snowy villages, and harbor towns on the Sea of Japan. Koshinetsu has many different ways of life, from the mountains to the sea.

People who live in towns and farming villages like these also move for reasons such as school, a new job, or family life.

In each of these towns and cities, where do people come from, and where do they go when they leave?

Why not trace the flows of people in each part of Koshinetsu by following the map?

## Why We Made the Koshinetsu Edition

“Are people from all over Koshinetsu gathering in Tokyo?” That question made us want to look more closely at the movement of people within Koshinetsu.

## Exploring Population Flow in Koshinetsu on the Map

This map lets you compare internal migration in Koshinetsu across 2019, 2021 and 2025.

Switch between sex, nationality and age group to see where people moved in from and moved out to, municipality by municipality. “Moving in” means changing your registered address to that area; “moving out” means changing it from that area to another area.

## What I Noticed on the Map

What stood out to me on the map of Koshinetsu was that, within each prefecture, people seemed to be moving toward the central city.

I also noticed more people coming in from outside Koshinetsu than I had expected.

Looking at the flows of people toward Tokyo, and also at people coming into Koshinetsu from other areas and people moving between nearby towns, reveals connections with many different areas.

In your country, what kinds of movements are there in towns surrounded by mountains, and in areas far from big cities?

## Net Migration for the Whole of Koshinetsu

How has the difference between people moving in and people moving out changed across the whole of Koshinetsu?

The data card below compares net migration for 2019, 2021 and 2025.

【Net Migration Data Card: Koshinetsu】

Net migration is the number of people who moved in minus the number who moved out. A positive figure means more people moved in than out (net inflow); a negative figure means more moved out than in (net outflow).

## About the Data

The totals and net migration figures shown here cover domestic moves by Japanese nationals and foreign nationals combined.

The map's age view uses four groups: 0–19, 20–39, 40–59 and 60 and over. People whose age is unknown are not included in these four groups.

This page is based on data published by the Statistics Bureau of Japan in its “Report on Internal Migration in Japan.” Visible World by Data compiled and tabulated the data and turned it into the map and the data card.

The years covered are 2019, 2021 and 2025. The Koshinetsu edition of this site covers three prefectures: Yamanashi, Nagano and Niigata.

The migration shown here means people who changed their registered address across municipal or similar boundaries within Japan. It does not show people entering Japan from abroad or leaving Japan for other countries.

It also differs from commuting, travel or tourist crowds, and from population change that includes births and deaths. If the same person moved more than once during the period, each move is counted.

The municipality-level data on the map include moves within the same prefecture. In the city of Niigata, a designated city, moves to a different ward within the same city are also counted.

The data card adds up the net migration of the three covered prefectures. Moves among the three prefectures cancel out to zero for the region as a whole, so the card shows the difference between moves in and out relative to places outside the region. This is a different measure from the municipal in-migration figures shown on the map or from the sum of the rankings.

### Notes on Reading the Figures

- “What I Noticed on the Map” records impressions from looking at the map and the figures. It is not a statistically verified conclusion about the reasons for moving or about regional trends.
- Migration alone cannot tell you whether a region's total population went up or down.
- The rankings cover the municipalities and wards listed individually in the official table. Areas grouped under “others” and similar rows are not ranked, so this is not a complete ranking of all municipalities.
- The total shown is the official table's total, not the sum of the top-10 ranking.
- The nationality switch shows people who moved within Japan, by nationality. It does not show movement across national borders.
- Where municipal boundaries or classifications have changed, comparisons across years need care.
